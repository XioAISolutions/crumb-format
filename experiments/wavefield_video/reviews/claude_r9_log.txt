## (1) The scale-dependent flaw

The collapse is **real** (not a metric artifact — the zero-init residual model literally starts at copy-last and `copy_ratio 0.115` means it stayed there), and the decisive fact is that **the benchmark scales field-of-view, not resolution.** `RADIUS=1.6` and `SPEED=1.15` are constants in grid-*cell* units, so g32 is the *same tiny balls* in a 4× larger, ~4× emptier canvas — `moving_frac` ~0.75 → ~0.19.

The 0.5/0.5 split makes the loss `moving_frac`-invariant **for its first two terms** — so collapse is *not* a loss optimum (perfect prediction still scores 0 at every grid); it's an **optimization** failure. Three leaks push toward freeze as the canvas empties:

1. **`0.25·resid` is full-frame/un-balanced** — its motion content dilutes ∝ `moving_frac`, re-importing the copy-last shortcut the split removed (its share of `L_init` falls 0.27→0.09).
2. **K-ramp × `--collisions`** — far-horizon ball positions are unpredictable (bounces), so those `e_move` gradients are zero-mean noise; freezing protects the now-larger static+resid mass across all 8 horizons, and it bites in the second half exactly as OneCycleLR decays. Benign at g16 (freezing is catastrophic at 75% moving), harmful at g32.
3. **Fixed compute for 4× more per-pixel tokens** — the honest confound.

I **ruled out** two of your three hypotheses: threshold scale-dependence (per-pixel |Δ| classification is grid-invariant in cell units) and kernel capacity (advection `vx` and damping are parameterized in cell units → grid-invariant).
