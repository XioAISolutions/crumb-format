`★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────`
Two spec subtleties drove the trickiest code. (1) **rfft2 asymmetry**: width holds only non-negative freqs (low band = plain `[:kw]` slice) but height wraps negative freqs to the end — so the low-band box needs `fftshift`→center-crop→`ifftshift`, and DC ends up at box row `kh//2`, not `[0,0]`. (2) **The 24-byte `dc_only` claim forces a 1×1 anchor** — I made the engine collapse the box to 1×1 in that mode, so `state_bytes` hits exactly 24 rather than approximately.

## One thing I want your judgment on (spec §3.2)

The spec explicitly calls `target_lowband` the **load-bearing decision** — "do not accept the default blindly." I shipped the three modes faithfully, but there's a genuine product fork the spec leaves open in `core.py`:

> for a **locked-camera** product you may want to also stabilize the *lowest phase ring* (freezing coarse layout jitter), whereas the current `magnitude` default keeps **all** phase free (right for **moving-camera** content).

Right now `magnitude` = `|anchor| · (Xlo/|Xlo|)` — 100% of phase stays free. If your brainsnn.com target is mostly static/orbiting shots, a variant that blends in a little low-ring phase anchoring could remove coarse layout wobble that pure-magnitude leaves in. That's a 3-5 line change to `target_lowband` and a real behavioral tradeoff. **Do you want the moving-camera default as-is, or should I add a `phase_lock_rings` knob for the locked-camera case before M1?**
